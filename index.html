Dr. Jost Blasberg

Postdoctoral Researcher in Psychology
Leipzig University & Jena University Hospital

I study how young people meet each other, how they share their experiences and feelings, and what happens between them while they do. Empathy sits at the centre of my work: how it develops across childhood and adolescence, and how sharing in another person's experience can be steered in a positive, supportive direction. With this, I hope to contribute to a deeper understanding of the foundations of prosocial behaviour.

A second focus is the use of digital technologies to study human experience and behaviour. Video, voice and wearable physiology open up new ways to capture social and emotional processes in fine detail and as they unfold over time.

Research

Empathy & empathic stress

Observing someone under stress can trigger a stress response in the observer. I examine when and how this happens, and what it means for empathy and helping.

Family stress

Parents and children share much of their emotional and physiological lives. I study how stress travels between mothers, fathers and their children and adolescents, in the lab and in everyday life.

Dynamic systems

I treat social interaction as coupled systems that move together over time, and model empathy as convergence of two people's affective states rather than as a single snapshot.

Multimodal measurement

I combine self-report, heart rate and HRV, cortisol, automated facial analysis and voice acoustics to capture how people feel and respond together.
